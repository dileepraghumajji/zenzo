# Zenzo Design Philosophy

## One Line
Zenzo feels like a consumer app that runs a business — not business software that consumers tolerate.

## Core Belief
The best tool is the one that disappears. A gym owner should think about their members, not their software. A coach should think about their class, not their screen. A member should think about their next session, not their next click.

## Visual Identity
- **Clean over clever** — No gradients, no decorative elements, no visual noise. Every pixel earns its place.
- **Warm over cold** — We're not a bank. We're the tool that helps people do what they love. Colours are warm, language is human, interactions feel alive.
- **Dense over sparse (for power users)** — Owners who want data should see data. But density must be organized, not overwhelming. Think Linear's issue list, not Jira's backlog.
- **Breathing room for beginners** — New users see generous whitespace, large text, clear CTAs. Complexity is earned, not imposed.

## The Three Tests
Every screen must pass:
1. **Ravi Test** — Can a non-technical gym owner in Tier 2 India figure out what to do next without asking anyone?
2. **Arjun Test** — Can a coach complete attendance in under 60 seconds on a mid-range Android phone?
3. **Stripe Test** — Would a designer at Linear or Stripe nod at this — not cringe?

## Design Decisions We've Made (and won't revisit)
1. Mobile-first for coaches. Desktop-first for owners. Both are intentional.
2. No dark mode in Phase 1 — adds complexity, low priority for target users, revisit after launch.
3. WhatsApp messages sound human, never robotic.
4. Empty states teach. They're not dead ends — they're onramps.
5. Attendance is a ritual, not a chore. It must feel satisfying.
6. The dashboard is a daily digest, not a control panel.
7. Progressive disclosure everywhere — show less, reveal more on demand.
8. Indian locale defaults — ₹, DD MMM YYYY, 12-hour time, en-IN number formatting.
